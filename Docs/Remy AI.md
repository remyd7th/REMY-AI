# **Remy AI**

## **Product Requirements Document (PRD)**

### **1\. Product Overview**

**Product Name:** Remy AI

**Product Type:** AI-powered Executive and Virtual Assistance platform

**Primary Users:** Executive Assistants (EAs) and Virtual Assistants (VAs)

**Secondary Users:** Small business owners, entrepreneurs, professionals, teams, and eventually larger organizations.

### **Product Vision**

Remy AI is an intelligent digital assistant designed to help Executive Assistants and Virtual Assistants manage and complete their daily responsibilities.

Remy combines **task management, scheduling, email assistance, document management, follow-ups, communication, payments, progress tracking, and personalized assistance** in one place.

Rather than functioning as another basic productivity application, Remy should feel like an **AI coworker** that helps users organize their workload, identify what needs attention, prepare work, and carry out approved actions.

### **Core Product Principle**

> **Remy helps the user organize, assist, suggest, and execute — while the user remains in control.**

---

# **2\. Problem Statement**

Executive Assistants and Virtual Assistants often manage many responsibilities at the same time.

Their work may involve:

* Managing calendars  
* Scheduling meetings  
* Responding to emails  
* Following up with clients  
* Preparing documents  
* Managing tasks  
* Tracking deadlines  
* Coordinating multiple people  
* Managing several clients or executives  
* Handling recurring administrative activities  
* Tracking payments and expenses  
* Reporting progress

These responsibilities are often spread across multiple applications and communication channels.

This creates problems such as:

* Important tasks being forgotten  
* Missed follow-ups  
* Schedule conflicts  
* Information being difficult to find  
* Repetitive administrative work  
* Difficulty knowing what should be prioritized  
* Spending too much time switching between different tools  
* Difficulty managing several clients or executives simultaneously

**Remy's goal is to bring these activities together and provide intelligent assistance throughout the user's workday.**

# **4\. Product Positioning**

Remy should not position itself simply as:

> "An AI chatbot."

> **"Your intelligent AI work assistant."**

The difference is that Remy does not only answer questions.

It helps users:

**Plan → Organize → Execute → Follow Up → Track → Improve**

---

# **5\. Core User Experience**

Remy will have three primary ways users interact with it:

### **1\. Chat**

Users can communicate with Remy naturally.

Examples:

> "Remy, organize my tasks for tomorrow."

> "Draft a follow-up email to Sarah."

> "Schedule a meeting with David next week."

> "What needs my attention today?"

### **2\. Voice**

Users can speak naturally to Remy when they are busy or do not want to type.

Example:

> "Remy, remind me to send the proposal tomorrow morning."

### **3\. Dashboard**

The dashboard provides a visual overview of the user's work.

It should help users quickly understand:

* Today's tasks  
* Upcoming meetings  
* Important emails  
* Pending follow-ups  
* Documents requiring attention  
* Payments  
* Overdue work  
* Progress  
* Client or executive activities

---

# **6\. Core Features**

## **6.1 Intelligent Task Management**

Remy should provide more than a basic to-do list.

Users should be able to:

* Create tasks  
* Edit tasks  
* Delete tasks  
* Assign tasks  
* Set deadlines  
* Set priorities  
* Create recurring tasks  
* Break large tasks into smaller steps  
* Track task completion  
* Identify overdue tasks  
* View upcoming tasks  
* Organize tasks by client, executive, project, or team

### **Intelligent Assistance**

Remy should analyze the user's workload and make suggestions.

Example:

> "You have eight tasks today. Two are overdue and one is connected to tomorrow's client meeting. Would you like me to help reorganize your workload?"

Remy should **suggest**, rather than make important decisions without the user's permission.

---

# **7\. Calendar & Scheduling**

Remy should function as a full scheduling assistant.

Users should be able to:

* Schedule meetings  
* Create appointments  
* Set reminders  
* Check availability  
* Identify scheduling conflicts  
* Suggest suitable meeting times  
* Coordinate schedules  
* Reschedule meetings  
* Cancel meetings  
* Send meeting invitations  
* Suggest meeting durations  
* Prepare meeting agendas

Example:

> "Remy, find a suitable time for a 45-minute meeting with Sarah next week."

Remy should review the relevant schedules and suggest suitable options.

---

# **8\. Email Assistant**

Email should be one of Remy's major capabilities.

### **Email Organization**

Remy should help users:

* Identify important emails  
* Highlight urgent emails  
* Summarize long emails  
* Identify emails requiring responses  
* Organize email priorities

### **Email Writing**

Remy should:

* Draft emails  
* Rewrite emails  
* Improve clarity  
* Adjust tone  
* Make emails shorter or more detailed  
* Create emails from simple instructions

Example:

> "Remy, write a professional but friendly response telling Sarah that we need to move the meeting to Friday."

### **Email Follow-Up**

Remy should:

* Identify unanswered emails  
* Track pending responses  
* Remind users about follow-ups  
* Suggest when a follow-up may be appropriate  
* Draft follow-up emails

---

# **9\. Follow-Up Management**

Follow-ups are a major part of administrative work.

Remy should help users track:

* Client follow-ups  
* Email responses  
* Meeting follow-ups  
* Outstanding requests

* Tasks completed  
* Tasks pending  
* Overdue tasks  
* Upcoming meetings  
* Important emails  
* Pending follow-ups  
* Documents requiring attention  
* Payment activities

### **Weekly Overview**

Remy can provide summaries such as:

> **Your Week**

> 32 tasks completed

> **"What is happening across my work, and what needs my attention?"**

# **15\. Personalized Memory**

Remy should remember useful work preferences.

Examples:

> "I prefer meetings after 10 AM."

> "Keep my emails short and professional."

> "My weekly report is due every Friday."

> "Don't schedule meetings during my lunch break."

Remy should also support different preferences for different clients or executives.

### **Example**

**My preferences**

* Short emails  
* Meetings after 10 AM

**Sarah — CEO**

* Prefers morning meetings  
* Formal email communication

**David — Client**

* Prefers afternoon meetings  
* Wants reminders one day before meetings

Users should be able to **view, edit and remove remembered preferences.**

# **16\. Permission & Approval System**

This is one of Remy's most important features.

Users should determine what Remy is allowed to do independently.

### **Example Permission Levels**

**Always allowed**

* Create tasks  
* Organize tasks  
* Create reminders  
* Summarize documents  
* Prepare drafts

**Ask before doing**

* Send emails  
* Schedule meetings  
* Send important communications  
* Share documents

**Require approval**

* Make significant payments  
* Cancel important appointments  
* Perform sensitive actions

Users should be able to change these permissions at any time.

---

# **17\. Remy's Proactive Assistance**

Remy should not wait for instructions all the time.

However, it should also avoid becoming intrusive.

The preferred model is:

### **Notice → Suggest → User decides**

Example:

> **Remy:** "You have a client meeting tomorrow, but there is no preparation task. Would you like me to create a meeting preparation checklist?"

Or:

> **Remy:** "You haven't received a response to the proposal you sent three days ago. Would you like me to prepare a follow-up?"

This gives Remy intelligence while keeping the user in control.

# **18\. Example User Journey**

### **Morning**

The user opens Remy.

Remy says:

> **Good morning. Here's what needs your attention today.**

* 3 priority tasks  
* 2 meetings  
* 4 important emails  
* 1 overdue follow-up  
* 1 document awaiting review

Remy then suggests:

> "Your 2 PM meeting requires preparation. Would you like me to create a preparation checklist?"

### **During the Day**

The user says:

> "Remy, schedule a meeting with David for next week."

Remy finds suitable times and presents options.

The user selects one.

Remy schedules the meeting according to the user's permission settings.

### **Later**

Remy notices an unanswered email:

> "John hasn't responded to the proposal sent three days ago. Would you like me to draft a follow-up?"

The user says:

> "Yes."

Remy prepares the email.

### **End of Day**

Remy provides:

> **Today's Progress**

> 7 tasks completed  
> 2 tasks remaining  
> 1 follow-up pending  
> 3 meetings completed  
> 5 important emails handled

It then suggests what should receive attention tomorrow.
